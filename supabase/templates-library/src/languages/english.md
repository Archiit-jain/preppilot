# A1 Breakthrough (Pre A1 Starters to A1 Movers)

## Listening

- Familiar words about yourself and your family
- Very basic phrases spoken slowly and clearly

## Reading

- Names, familiar words and simple sentences
- Notices, posters and catalogues

## Speaking

- Simple exchanges with a patient partner
- Asking and answering simple questions on immediate needs
- Describing where you live and people you know

## Writing

- A short postcard or greeting
- Filling in forms with personal details

# A2 Waystage (A2 Key)

## Listening

- High-frequency phrases on personal topics: family, shopping, local area, work
- The main point of short, clear messages and announcements

## Reading

- Very short, simple texts
- Finding information in adverts, menus and timetables
- Short simple personal letters

## Speaking

- Routine tasks and simple exchanges of information
- Very short social exchanges
- Describing family, living conditions, education and work

## Writing

- Short notes and messages
- A very simple personal letter

# B1 Threshold (B1 Preliminary)

## Listening

- Main points of clear standard speech on familiar matters
- Radio and TV on current affairs when delivery is slow and clear

## Reading

- Texts in everyday or job-related language
- Descriptions of events, feelings and wishes in personal letters

## Speaking

- Handling most situations while travelling
- Unprepared conversation on familiar topics
- Describing experiences, dreams and ambitions
- Giving reasons for opinions and plans
- Narrating a story or the plot of a book or film

## Writing

- Simple connected text on familiar topics
- Personal letters describing experiences and impressions

# B2 Vantage (B2 First)

## Listening

- Extended speech and lectures on familiar topics
- TV news, current affairs and most films

## Reading

- Articles and reports on contemporary problems
- Contemporary literary prose

## Speaking

- Fluent, spontaneous interaction with native speakers
- Taking part in discussions and defending your views
- Clear, detailed descriptions on many subjects
- Explaining a viewpoint with advantages and disadvantages

## Writing

- Clear, detailed text on a wide range of subjects
- Essays and reports arguing for or against a view
- Letters highlighting the significance of events

# C1 Effective Operational Proficiency (C1 Advanced)

## Listening

- Extended speech that is not clearly structured
- TV programmes and films without much effort

## Reading

- Long, complex factual and literary texts and their style
- Specialised articles and technical instructions

## Speaking

- Fluent, spontaneous expression without searching for words
- Flexible use of language for social and professional purposes
- Clear descriptions of complex subjects with a rounded conclusion

## Writing

- Well-structured text expressing points of view at length
- Letters, essays and reports on complex subjects
- Choosing a style suited to the reader

# C2 Mastery (C2 Proficiency)

## Listening

- Any kind of spoken language, including fast native speech

## Reading

- Virtually all written language, including abstract and complex texts
- Manuals, specialised articles and literary works

## Speaking

- Effortless participation in any conversation
- Idiomatic expressions and colloquialisms
- Conveying fine shades of meaning precisely
- Smooth, well-structured descriptions and arguments

## Writing

- Clear, smoothly flowing text in an appropriate style
- Complex letters, reports and articles
- Summaries and reviews of professional or literary works
